How the Market projection is calculated

Betting lines are the only real market data available without a paid subscription. They price the game, not the player, so this column takes a player's recent form and re-prices it for the game environment the market expects.

  1. Read the closing spread and total for each game from nflverse ({{ market.games_priced }} of this week's games priced).
  2. Split them into each side's implied team total: total ÷ 2 ± spread ÷ 2.
  3. Compare that to the week's average implied total to get an environment multiplier, capped to 0.75–1.30.
  4. Multiply the player's decayed 4-game fantasy average by it.

Read it with these in mind

The line prices the team, not the player. Every player on a side gets the same multiplier. It cannot know that one receiver is expected to absorb the targets and another is not.

The baseline is backward-looking. Recent scoring carries a player's own past usage into it, so a changed role shows up only as those games age into the 4-game window.

A defense is scaled by the opponent's total, inverted — a defense facing a low-scoring offense is in a good spot.

The multiplier is capped at 0.75–1.30. A line can imply a genuinely extreme game, but the market is not claiming one player's output doubles.

Why not real player props?

Props (passing yards, receptions, anytime TD) would be the better signal, and the Sportsbook column is wired for them. Every provider checked gates NFL props behind a paid plan, so that column has no data and contributes nothing. It is left in place so it starts working the moment real props are available.
